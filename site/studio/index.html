(The architect)

A personal practice.

Carlos Vinocour

Founder & Architect

  • Architect, CFIA A-31886
  • Over 10 years of experience

Carlos Vinocour is a Costa Rican architect with over a decade of experience in residential and hospitality design, particularly along the country's Pacific coast.

His approach begins with listening. Understanding how clients live, what matters to them, and what they envision for their future becomes the foundation for each project.

Carlos believes exceptional architecture begins with the quality of space itself. Natural light, thoughtful proportions, material warmth, and a meaningful relationship with the landscape create comfort and a sense of understated luxury.

Through Studio CAVA, clients work directly with Carlos during the design process, benefiting from personal attention, clear communication, and guidance informed by years of experience in Costa Rica.

(Manifesto)

What webelieve

Five ideas we hold to on every project.

Climate

The climate draws first

Before there is a floor plan there is a sun path. We note where the afternoon sun hits, where the breeze comes from in the dry season and where the water runs in the rainy one, and the house takes its shape from that.

A house that needs air conditioning all day was designed for somewhere else. Deep roofs, shaded glass and rooms open on two sides use less power and are easier to live in.

Place

The lot is the brief

We walk every lot twice, in the morning and in the afternoon, before drawing a line. The slope, the big trees, the rock, the view and the neighbours decide more than any style does.

The lot is the one part of a project that cannot be changed later. A house that follows the slope moves less earth, keeps more trees and sits better on the hill.

Time

Built to age, not to wear out

Salt air, humidity and six months of rain are hard on any building. We choose materials that age instead of wearing out: teak, board-formed concrete, local stone, steel galvanised for the coast.

A house is lived in for decades and its maintenance is paid every year. We would rather spend on the roof and on the details that keep water out than on finishes that look tired after two rainy seasons.

Detail

One set of drawings, from the roof to the shelves

We draw the inside with the outside: kitchens, built-in furniture, lighting, the handrail and the joint where the timber meets the stone. The same materials run from the facade to the shelves.

Most of what people touch every day is a detail. Details left to be solved on site get solved in a hurry.

Clarity

No surprises in cost or time

Building in Costa Rica has its own order: land use, then design, then permits through the CFIA and the municipality. We follow it, we tell you how long each step usually takes, and every stage ends with drawings you approve.

Design fees are quoted per project, in writing, after the first meeting. If we are not the right studio for your project, we say so at that meeting.

(Clarity, in practice)

Informed decisions, at every stage.

Every site presents possibilities, along with questions that deserve careful attention. Our role is not to promise a project without challenges, but to identify potential issues early, coordinate the right expertise, and help you understand your options before making important commitments.

We believe good communication is as important as good drawings. Clear deliverables, transparent responsibilities, and well-defined decisions create a stronger foundation for the entire project.

(Our process)

From first conversation to built reality.

Designing a home in Costa Rica involves creative decisions, technical coordination, and regulatory requirements. Our process is organized into clearly defined stages, giving you the information and guidance needed to make informed decisions as the project develops.

  1. (01)

    Discovery

    We discuss your property, aspirations, priorities, intended use, approximate budget, and expectations.

    You receive A preliminary understanding of the project and a written proposal defining services, scope, fees, and next steps.

    Book a free call →

  2. (02)

    Site feasibility and due diligence

    Before making major design commitments, we walk the lot with you and assess the property's opportunities and constraints. Depending on the agreed scope, this may include:

    • Land-use regulations and municipal requirements
    • Setbacks, heights, coverage and density
    • Road access and relevant alignments
    • Water availability
    • Topography and site conditions
    • Environmental restrictions
    • Condominium regulations, when they apply
    • A preliminary buildable area
    • The additional technical studies the project will need

    Legal title and ownership due diligence require a lawyer, and technical studies are coordinated with qualified specialists. We do not guarantee that a property is suitable or that a permit will be approved.

    You receive A feasibility assessment outlining the relevant findings, the outstanding risks, and recommended next steps.

    Lot study →

  3. (03)

    Concept design

    We explore how the project could inhabit its site: layout, orientation, natural light, ventilation, access, views, and the relationships between spaces. Usually two or three ways of placing it on the lot; you pick one, or a mix.

    You receive Concept plans, sections, architectural studies and presentation material for your review.

  4. (04)

    Design development

    The selected direction evolves into a more resolved architectural proposal: proportions, materials, spatial relationships, the interiors, and what each engineering discipline will need.

    You receive Developed drawings, design decisions and preliminary cost considerations appropriate to the contracted scope.

  5. (05)

    Permits and approvals

    We prepare the drawings the CFIA and the municipality ask for, file them and follow them through review, along with environmental agencies, utilities and condominium review bodies where they apply. Some consultations, such as water availability or road alignments, begin earlier and run alongside the design.

    You receive Organized submissions, review responses and progress updates. Permit timing and approval remain subject to each authority.

    Building permits →

  6. (06)

    Technical documentation

    We coordinate the architectural design with the structural, electrical and mechanical engineers and prepare the documentation a builder can price and build from.

    You receive Drawings, details, schedules, and specifications, according to the contracted services.

    Architectural design →

  7. (07)

    Construction support

    Where contracted, we help you select the contractor, answer design questions during construction, visit the site to review the work, and keep the architectural intent. The contractor remains responsible for the construction; CAVA is not the general contractor.

    You receive The documentation and site visits agreed in the professional contract.

    Construction supervision →

Services and deliverables are tailored to each project and defined in the professional agreement. Certain studies, engineering services, approvals, and construction-phase responsibilities may be contracted separately.
