Buildingpermits

A house needs a land use certificate, water, the CFIA's platform and a municipal licence. Larger buildings, fragile sites and businesses add SETENA, the Ministry of Health, the Fire Department and more. This is the order, with the law or the office behind each step.

Reviewed October 2026. General information, not legal advice: rules change, and each municipality applies them its own way.

(Before you buy)

What the lot already decides

A municipality cannot issue a permit for a lot without an approved survey plan or without access to a public road, or for a project that does not fit the zoning in use, setbacks or coverage 1.

Fewer than half of Costa Rica's cantons have a plan regulador for their whole territory: 44 of 84 have one, and 22 of those cover only part of the canton. In Guanacaste, Abangares, Bagaces, Carrillo, Hojancha, La Cruz and Nandayure have none, and Liberia, Cañas, Nicoya, Santa Cruz and Tilarán have partial plans from the 1980s 21. Where there is no plan, the national construction rules apply 2.

  • Near water, the forestry law protects a 100 m radius around permanent springs, a strip of 15 m in the countryside and 10 m in town along rivers on flat land, 50 m on broken ground (an average slope over 40%), and 50 m around lakes 4,5.
  • Within 200 m of the high-tide line, the maritime zone law applies (see the coast, below) 25.
  • Inside a condominium or a tourism pole such as Papagayo, there is a second set of rules and approvals: see our guide to gated communities and the Papagayo page.

(A house)

The steps for a single-family house

  1. (01)

    Land use certificate

    From the municipality. It states, at least, the permitted use, setbacks, coverage, density, height, frontage and minimum lot area, and it lasts as long as the plan regulador does not change 1,2.

  2. (02)

    Alignments

    From MOPT if the lot faces a national road (up to 3 months), from the municipality for a cantonal road, and from INVU for rivers and springs (15 working days, valid 24 months) 3,4,5,13.

  3. (03)

    Water availability

    From AyA, the local ASADA or a well concession. Every building needs it. AyA issues it free within 15 working days, valid 12 months and extendable to 24 2,6.

  4. (04)

    Electricity

    Not a national requirement, but some municipalities ask for it: Carrillo asks for a letter from Coopeguanacaste or ICE 7.

  5. (05)

    SETENA, only when it applies

    Over 1,000 m², or 500 to 1,000 m² on a fragile site. The environmental licence must come before the permit 8. See below.

  6. (06)

    Drawings and contract in the CFIA platform

    The design contract is registered in the CFIA's APC platform, and the CFIA charges about 0.27% of its own valuation of the work. The CFIA stamp lasts one year 10,11.

  7. (07)

    Review in the APC

    For a house, the Ministry of Health approves on the professional's sworn declaration, and the Fire Department does not review it, though the house must still meet the fire rules 12.

  8. (08)

    Municipal licence

    The municipality charges a construction tax of up to 1% of the value of the work, and the licence needs the work-risk insurance policy first 1,13,14.

  9. (09)

    Digital site logbook

    Opened by the director of works, with its QR code visible on site, and closed within 30 days of finishing 15.

  10. (10)

    When the work ends

    Notify the municipality: occupying before giving notice is an infraction. There is no national occupancy certificate; some municipalities run a final inspection 13.

(Hotels and businesses)

What changes for a hotel, a shop or a restaurant

In the APC, the Ministry of Health and the Fire Department review every building other than a house: 15 working days for the first review and 5 for corrections, or 7 and 3 for simple shops and offices 12. For buildings of 300 m² or less, Health accepts a sworn declaration instead of its review, except for treatment plants, wastewater systems, radiation shielding and boilers 12.

The Fire Department has no size threshold for non-residential buildings, and reviews apartment buildings from 4 units up. Its regulation adopts the NFPA codes, NFPA 101 (2021) among them 12,18.

Buildings open to the public must meet the accessibility law: at least one accessible entrance, doors of 0.90 m, corridors of 1.20 m, ramps of 8 to 12% depending on their length, accessible toilets and 5% of parking, never fewer than two spaces. Without it, the permit is not processed 19,20.

  1. (01)

    Health operating permit

    After construction, the Permiso Sanitario de Funcionamiento. For most risk groups it is issued on submission, and it lasts 5 years 16.

  2. (02)

    Municipal business licence

    The patente: the municipality has 30 calendar days to decide, and it cannot be granted without the land use certificate 1,17.

(SETENA)

When SETENA's environmental review applies

Since 20 August 2023, the threshold is the same for houses, hotels, shops and residential buildings 8,9:

Built areaOn an ordinary siteOn a fragile site
Under 500 m²No SETENANo SETENA
500 to 1,000 m²No SETENAForm D1-C, resolved within 7 days
Over 1,000 m²Form D1Form D1

Earthworks follow the same thresholds in cubic metres 8. Fragile sites include the maritime zone, forest, rivers and springs with their protection strips, aquifer recharge areas, high-hazard areas, protected areas, heritage sites and indigenous territories 8.

The 500 m² rule many people still quote came from the previous regulation, Decreto 31849, which was repealed in 2023 8,9. The new thresholds are being challenged before the Constitutional Court, and the Procuraduría has said the change lacks technical support, so they could change 22,23.

After a D1, the route depends on the project's environmental impact score: a sworn declaration of environmental commitments, an environmental management plan or a full impact study. Fees run from US$60 for a D1-C to US$1,500 for a full study, and the licence lasts 5 years before works start 8.

In Guanacaste, where several coastal aquifers are under pressure, SENARA's matrix limits density and paved area on highly vulnerable land; how binding it is has been argued in the courts 24.

(The coast)

Building near the beach

The maritime zone is the 200 m strip from the ordinary high-tide line. The first 50 m are the public zone, which cannot be occupied under any title; building there illegally can bring demolition without compensation and prison 25.

In the remaining 150 m, the restricted zone, only municipalities grant concessions, with the ICT's approval in tourist areas, for 5 to 20 years and against a yearly fee. A permanent building needs a registered concession that fits an approved coastal plan; a mere use permit allows only works that are easy to remove 25,26.

Concessions cannot go to foreigners with less than 5 years of residence, or to companies more than half owned by foreigners 25,26. Coastal plans are still scarce: in a 2026 survey, only 6 of the 13 coastal cantons that answered had one for some of their beaches, and a 2025 law gave municipalities without plans 8 more years 27,28.

In practice the municipality makes the difference. In the CFIA's 2025 ranking, approving a permit in the APC took on average about 1 day in Escazú, 10 in Liberia and Santa Cruz, 12 in Santa Ana, 19 in Garabito, 26 in Carrillo and 35 in Quepos; Liberia approved 61% of files on the first submission, Santa Cruz 35% and Escazú 16% 29.

The most common reasons for sending a file back, in a Contraloría audit, were setbacks, windows on boundary walls, room sizes and ventilation, and missing documents such as the land use certificate 30.

(Questions)

Do I need SETENA to build a house?

Not if it is under 1,000 m², unless it measures 500 to 1,000 m² and sits on a fragile site such as the maritime zone, forest, a river's protection strip or an aquifer recharge area, in which case it files a D1-C 8.

How much does a building permit cost?

The municipal construction tax is up to 1% of the value of the work, and the CFIA charges about 0.27% of its valuation. Add the work-risk insurance, SETENA's fee when it applies, and the condominium's review fees if there is one 1,11,14,8.

How long does it take?

The legal limits are short (15 working days for most reviews), but municipalities vary: from about a day in Escazú to over a month in Quepos in the CFIA's 2025 ranking. SETENA, alignments and a condominium's review add their own time 12,29.

Is there an occupancy permit at the end?

Not a national one. The owner must tell the municipality the work is finished before using it, and some municipalities inspect. A business also needs the Health operating permit and the municipal licence 13,16,17.

Can a foreigner build near the beach?

Not in the 50 m public zone. In the restricted zone, concessions cannot go to foreigners with less than 5 years of residence or to companies more than half foreign-owned 25,26.
