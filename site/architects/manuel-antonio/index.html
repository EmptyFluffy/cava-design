(The year in Manuel Antonio)

The dry season runs from February to March: 2 months under 60 mm of rain. The wettest month is September, with 623 mm.

Afternoons are hottest in March, at 27 °C on average, and coolest in December, at 25 °C. Nights stay between 22 and 23 °C.

Best time to start*

The best time to start building here is from December to March. Those 4 months bring 323 mm of rain in total; the other 8 bring 3,649 mm, 11 times as much.

Earthworks, foundations and concrete pours need dry ground, and many roads to the lots only stay passable without rain. On a day with 10 mm of rain or more, that work usually stops.

A 4-month phase of earthworks, foundations and structure that starts in December meets about 4 such days here; the same phase started in April meets about 29. Starting in December saves about 25 days of stopped work: some 4 weeks of crew and machinery paid to wait, and the pumping and drainage that wet excavations need. So the permit should be ready by November. Still, it is a recommendation, not a rule: building goes on through the rains, more slowly, and no project should sit for months waiting for the dry season.

(The wind in Manuel Antonio)

In the dry season the afternoon wind at Parrita comes from the southwest.

Rooms that open to the wind on one side and away from it on the other cool themselves, and a roof that sheds the rain lets them stay open in the wet months too.

(The sun in Manuel Antonio)

The sun here is never far from overhead. At noon it stands 80° high in March and September and 57° in December, and from April 15 to August 29 it passes north of overhead, so walls and glass that face north take sun as well.

What heats a house is the low afternoon sun. It sets at 294°, west-northwest, in June and at 246°, west-southwest, in December. That side needs the deepest roof.

Sunrise falls between 5:15 and 5:58 all year, and sunset between 17:12 and 18:01.

The ground here gets 1,958 kWh of sunlight per m² a year, and solar panels make about 1,582 kWh a year for each kW installed.

(Building in Manuel Antonio)

Which permits you need to build in Manuel Antonio, and in what order

The building permit in Manuel Antonio comes from the Municipalidad de Quepos. These are the four steps every project takes; for the whole route of yours, with SETENA, Health and the Fire Department, use the permit route tool.

Draw your route in the permit route tool →

  1. (01)

    Land use certificate

    The uso de suelo, from the Municipalidad de Quepos: what the zoning allows on the lot.

  2. (02)

    Water availability letter

    From the local ASADA or from AyA. Without it there is no building permit, so it is worth asking for before buying.

  3. (03)

    Drawings at the CFIA

    Filed through the CFIA's APC platform and signed by the architect in charge.

  4. (04)

    Building permit

    Issued by the Municipalidad de Quepos.

Within 200 m of the ordinary high-tide line the maritime zone law (Ley 6043) applies: the first 50 m are public and cannot be built on, and the next 150 m are held under a concession from the municipality, not owned. Check where a lot sits before you buy.

(Questions about building in Manuel Antonio)

Where is Studio CAVA based, and how do you work in Manuel Antonio?

We work from San José and Guanacaste. We visit Manuel Antonio for the analysis of the lot and, during construction, for the supervision visits; the rest happens by video and email, in English or Spanish.

Can you design my house in Manuel Antonio if I live abroad?

Yes. The first call is free and by video, and each stage of the design ends with drawings you review and approve at a distance. We walk the lot in Manuel Antonio ourselves, and during construction we send a report with photos after each visit.

How much does it cost to build a house in Manuel Antonio?

A 250 m² house with a pool comes to about US$639,000 to US$987,000 in Manuel Antonio, with a high finish, design, permits and VAT. Our cost estimator gives the range for your own size, finish and lot, with the schedule month by month.

What should I check before buying a lot in Manuel Antonio?

The land use certificate from the Municipalidad de Quepos, which sets the use, setbacks, coverage and height; water availability in writing from AyA, the local ASADA or a well; the setbacks from any river or spring; whether the lot is within 200 m of the high-tide line, where the maritime zone law applies; and the slope. Our lot study checks it in 2 to 4 weeks, before you sign.

When is the best time to start building in Manuel Antonio?

Ideally from December to March, so that the earthworks, foundations and structure fall in the dry season. A 4-month phase of that work started in December loses about 4 days to heavy rain, against about 29 if it starts in April, about 25 days of crew and machinery. It is a recommendation, not a rule: building goes on through the rains, more slowly, and no project should sit for months waiting for the dry season. To start then, have the permit ready by November.

Who issues building permits in Manuel Antonio?

The Municipalidad de Quepos. The drawings are filed first with the CFIA, through its APC platform, and the permit comes after the land use certificate and the water availability letter.

Can I build near the beach in Manuel Antonio?

Not in the first 50 m from the ordinary high-tide line, which are public. The next 150 m are held under a concession from the municipality rather than owned, with their own rules. Beyond 200 m, a titled lot follows the normal permit process.

How hot does it get in Manuel Antonio?

Afternoons average 27 °C in March, the hottest month, and 25 °C in December. Nights stay between 22 and 23 °C. Shade on the west side and air moving through the rooms do more for comfort than anything else.

How far is Manuel Antonio from the airports?

By road, without traffic, 4 h 55 min from Daniel Oduber airport in Liberia (265 km) and 3 h 5 min from Juan Santamaría airport in San José (153 km).
