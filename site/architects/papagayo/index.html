(The year)

The dry season runs from December to April: 5 months under 60 mm of rain. The wettest month is October, with 426 mm.

July rains less than June and August: the short break known as the veranillo de San Juan.

Afternoons are hottest in April, at 34 °C on average, and coolest in November, at 30 °C. Nights stay between 23 and 26 °C.

Best time to build

The best time to build here is from December to April. Those 5 months bring 54 mm of rain in total; the other 7 bring 1,739 mm, 32 times as much.

Earthworks, foundations and concrete pours need dry ground, and many roads to the lots only stay passable without rain. On a day with 10 mm of rain or more, that work usually stops.

A 4-month phase of earthworks, foundations and structure that starts in December meets almost no such days here; the same phase started in May meets about 27. Starting in December saves about 27 days of stopped work: some 4 weeks of crew and machinery paid to wait, and the pumping and drainage that wet excavations need. So the permit should be ready by November.

(The wind)

In the dry season the afternoon wind at Liberia airport comes from the northeast.

Rooms that open to the wind on one side and away from it on the other cool themselves, and a roof that sheds the rain lets them stay open in the wet months too.

(Wind and sun)

(The sun)

The sun here is never far from overhead. At noon it stands 79° high in March and September and 56° in December, and from April 19 to August 26 it passes north of overhead, so walls and glass that face north take sun as well.

What heats a house is the low afternoon sun. It sets at 294°, west-northwest, in June and at 246°, west-southwest, in December. That side needs the deepest roof.

Sunrise falls between 5:19 and 6:06 all year, and sunset between 17:17 and 18:09.

The ground here gets 2,097 kWh of sunlight per m² a year, and solar panels make about 1,690 kWh a year for each kW installed.

(Permits in Papagayo)

Building in the Golfo de Papagayo tourism pole

Most of the land around Bahía Culebra, Península Papagayo included, is not private property. It is state land run by the ICT, the national tourism institute, under Ley 6758, and owners hold a concession of up to 50 years: every right of an owner except selling the land 1,2. The coast inside the pole is under the ICT as well 3.

What can be built is set by the ICT's Plan Maestro: at most 14 m high from natural ground, an average of 20 rooms per hectare counting hotel and residential rooms alike, at most 30% of the original concession built over, and no septic tanks, since every project needs a treatment plant 4. The ICT also rules on colours, earth tones on at least 90% of visible facades, and on how much each roof or paving material counts toward the 30% 5,6.

Inside Península Papagayo a private board, the Master Design Review Board (MDRB), reviews every house first, under the Master Design Guidelines 7,8.

  1. (01)

    MDRB, in Península Papagayo

    A pre-design meeting, a schematic review and a final design review. Final approval comes before the ICT, the CFIA and the municipality. Each review costs US$2,000, plus a compliance deposit of US$20,000 to US$50,000 by house size 7.

  2. (02)

    SETENA, in parallel

    Environmental viability where the project's size and site require it. The ICT asks for the SETENA file number before it stamps the drawings 2,9.

  3. (03)

    ICT, the schematic project

    The ICT's executive office approves the anteproyecto: site design, description, phases, budget and schedule 2.

  4. (04)

    ICT, the final project

    The full construction drawings, with water demand, the wastewater plant, the fire network and storm drains. The ICT must resolve a complete file within 30 calendar days and then stamps the drawings 2.

  5. (05)

    CFIA and the municipality

    Then the usual route: the CFIA's APC platform and the building permit, from the Municipalidad de Liberia on the peninsula, or from Carrillo on the Playa Panamá side 2,10.

  6. (06)

    Build within the deadline

    For residential use, the house must be built within one fifth of the concession's original term 2.

  • Check the lot in the ICT's concession registry and on its concession map before buying: some boundaries and registry overlaps are under review 11.
  • Since April 2026 the Constitutional Court has suspended tree-felling permits for hotel projects in the pole, and construction authorizations that used the density compensation rule, which the government repealed in June 2026. The Procuraduría has also recommended annulling the 20 rooms per hectare and 30% rules. Check where both cases stand before committing 12,13,14,16.
  • Water has held projects back here before: in 2017, nine concessions were stalled for lack of a water availability letter. Inside Península Papagayo the resort operator supplies water, with a US$25,000 connection fee 7,15.
  • Every transfer or mortgage of a concession needs the ICT board's approval 1,2.

(Questions)

When is the best time to build in Papagayo?

From December to April, the dry season: 54 mm of rain in 5 months. A 4-month phase of earthworks, foundations and structure started in December loses almost no days to heavy rain, against about 27 if it starts in May, so a dry-season start saves about 27 days of crew and machinery. Have the permit ready by November.

Who issues building permits in Papagayo?

Several bodies, in order. Inside Península Papagayo the Master Design Review Board approves the design first; then the ICT approves the schematic and the final project and stamps the drawings; then the CFIA and the Municipalidad de Liberia (or Carrillo, on the Playa Panamá side) issue the usual permits. SETENA's environmental review runs in parallel.

Can I build near the beach in Papagayo?

The 50 m public zone stays public, and the land behind it is held under ICT concessions rather than municipal ones. What each lot allows is set by the ICT's Plan Maestro and, in Península Papagayo, by the lot's diagram in the design guidelines.

How hot does it get in Papagayo?

Afternoons average 34 °C in April, the hottest month, and 30 °C in November. Nights stay between 23 and 26 °C. Shade on the west side and air moving through the rooms do more for comfort than anything else.

How far is Papagayo from the airports?

By road, without traffic, 55 min from Daniel Oduber airport in Liberia (32 km) and 4 h 45 min from Juan Santamaría airport in San José (238 km).
